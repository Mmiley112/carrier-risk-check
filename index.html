Manual verification

The record clears the number. It can't clear the person.

A clean FMCSA record confirms the carrier's eligibility to operate. It does not confirm that the person or company contacting you is who they claim to be. Run these before tendering, every time.

Verify the identity

  1. Call the phone number in the federal record.Not the number in the email signature — the one FMCSA has on file.
  2. Compare the email domain with the carrier's known business identity.A mismatch is one of the most common double-brokering tells.
  3. Confirm the dispatcher and booked contact.Names swapped mid-booking are a pressure tactic, not a coincidence.

Verify the truck

  1. Confirm tractor, trailer, and driver information.Match it against what's on file, not just what's texted to you.
  2. Compare pickup equipment with what was booked.Substitutions at pickup are a common last-mile fraud vector.
  3. Investigate unexplained substitutions.A reasonable explanation exists. Get it before the truck leaves.

Verify the money

  1. Confirm insurance through an independent source.Not a certificate emailed to you — call the carrier directly.
  2. Question last-minute factoring or remit-to changes.This is the single most common double-brokering red flag.
  3. Treat a rate far below the lane as a reason to verify, not a bargain.Underpriced capacity is bait, not luck.
How I classify risk

Three tiers. Not every flag means the same thing.

This is my own ranking, built from how these failures actually show up in freight — not a generic scoring model. A hard stop and a reason to double-check are not the same category, and treating them the same is how real fraud gets missed.

01 / STOP

Federal hard stop

The load does not move until the federal prohibition or eligibility failure is resolved. No exceptions.

  • Active out-of-service order
  • Required operating authority is not active
  • Carrier explicitly not authorized for the movement
02 / VERIFY

Verification trigger

Not an automatic rejection. Confirm the carrier, equipment, coverage, and load fit before tendering.

  • Carrier is unfamiliar and freshly bidding
  • Single-truck / single-driver on a high-value load
  • Time pressure on the tender itself
03 / CONTEXT

Administrative note

Worth noticing, but not strong enough to determine the decision by itself — unless it's paired with a Tier 1 failure.

  • MCS-150 filing appears overdue
  • Small fleet size, by itself
  • No inspection history for a genuinely new entrant

Set up your free FMCSA key

FMCSA's QCMobile API is free and requires no billing information — just a government WebKey. Takes about two minutes.

  1. Create a free account at login.gov if you don't already have one.
  2. Sign in at FMCSA's QCMobile developer portal and request a WebKey. It's issued instantly — no billing, no approval wait.
