How this is scored (methodology)
Tier 1 — Hard stop

Active out-of-service order. Non-negotiable. The carrier is federally barred from operating, full stop.

No active operating authority on file, for an interstate for-hire move. Without authority, the move itself isn't legal — this outranks a simple "not authorized" flag because it means there's no authority to fall back on at all.

Explicitly listed as not authorized to operate. Same failure category, called out separately because it can trip even when an authority technically exists on paper.

Tier 2 — Verify before tendering

Carrier is new and freshly bidding your lane. A serious trigger for manual verification — not an automatic pass or fail.

Fleet size doesn't match the load. A one-truck, one-driver operation taking high-value freight is a capacity and control mismatch worth confirming, not a disqualifier by itself.

Tier 3 — Low signal

Overdue MCS-150 filing. On its own this is administrative lag, not fraud — it's the lowest-weight signal in this model. It only matters if the underlying authority has actually gone inactive, and if that's true, Tier 1 already caught it.

This ranking reflects which failures make a move illegal versus which ones just call for a closer look before booking. Built from firsthand logistics-analyst judgment, not a generic checklist.

Manual verification checklist

FMCSA data catches a stale or revoked authority. It can't see who you're actually emailing. Run these every time, regardless of what the check above shows.

  • Call the carrier back on the phone number on file with FMCSA — not the number in the load board message or email signature.
  • Check the dispatch email domain against the carrier's legal name. A Gmail/Yahoo address dispatching for an established fleet is a mismatch worth a phone call.
  • Be suspicious of a "new" remit-to or factoring company introduced after the load is already booked — this is the single most common moment double-brokering surfaces.
  • Cross-check the truck and driver that show up at pickup against what was booked. A different tractor or an unfamiliar driver with no explanation is a red flag on its own.
  • If the carrier is brand new to you and bidding well under lane average on a high-value load, treat the low price as a flag, not a win.
